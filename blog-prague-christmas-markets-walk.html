A self-guided walk through Prague's Christmas markets

✎
Written by the Urban Tales team
November 23, 2026 · 5 min read

Prague runs two flagship Christmas markets at once, half a kilometer apart, open the same hours on the same dates — and most visitors only ever find the one in front of their hotel. This walk connects both, through the one stretch of the city most people cross without looking up.

Old Town Square's market and the one on Wenceslas Square both open on November 28, 2026, and both run daily from 10am to 10pm until January 6, 2027. The Old Town tree goes up first: it's lit the evening the market opens, usually around 4:30pm, with carols and a crowd packed shoulder to shoulder under the astronomical clock. Everything below is the walk between the two squares — about 20 minutes on foot, longer with stalls.

The clock everyone's actually watching

Start at the astronomical clock on the Old Town Hall tower, ringed this time of year by wooden stalls selling mulled wine and roasted ham. The clock itself is over 600 years old, and even in market season, a crowd still gathers under it on the hour to watch its small mechanical figures move — Death tips an hourglass, a rooster crows, and nothing about it has anything to do with Christmas, which hasn't stopped it from becoming the market's unofficial centerpiece anyway.

✎
Tip

Skip the mulled wine stall right under the clock — it's the most crowded one on the square. With Urban Tales running, the clock's full story plays as you reach it, so you can step back from the crowd and still catch it.

Where the stalls thin out

Leave the square along Celetná, the old coronation route, and the crowd drops fast — this street has shop windows, not stalls. It ends at the Powder Gate, a blackened Gothic tower that marks where Old Town used to stop. Step through it and the building on your right, Municipal House, turns out to sit on the site of Bohemia's medieval Royal Court; its Mayor's Hall is covered floor to ceiling in paintings by Alfons Mucha, visible only on a guided tour, not from the street.

The moat that's now a shopping street

From the Powder Gate, Na Příkopě runs straight toward Wenceslas Square. The name means "at the moat" — a defensive ditch ran along here until the 18th century, when Prague filled it in and built over it. None of that history is visible now; what you'll notice instead is the street strung with lights for the length of it, which makes the walk itself feel like part of the decorations rather than a gap between two markets.

The second market, and the statue above it

Na Příkopě opens straight into the bottom of Wenceslas Square and its second market, usually a little less crowded than Old Town's and, by most accounts, slightly cheaper for the same cup of svařák — Czech mulled wine, distinct from medovina, a sweeter, stronger hot honey wine sold at the same stalls. Walk up the square's gentle slope toward the equestrian statue of St. Wenceslas at the top. Praguers call this spot "pod ocasem" — under the tail — and for ten days in November 1989 it was the city's unofficial town square during the Velvet Revolution; this December, it's ringed by stalls instead of protesters, which is its own kind of Prague continuity.
