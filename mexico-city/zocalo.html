Three stories most visitors miss

1

A pedestal with no statue

In the 19th century a monument to independence was planned for the center of the square, but only its base, the zócalo, was ever built. The name stuck, and today squares all over Mexico are called zócalos.

2

A cathedral built over 250 years

The Metropolitan Cathedral took from 1573 to 1813 to finish, which is why it mixes Renaissance, Baroque and Neoclassical styles. It's the largest cathedral in the Americas.

3

The cry from the balcony

Every 15 September at night, the president rings the bell from the National Palace balcony and leads the "Grito" of independence to a square packed with people.

Zócalo (Plaza de la Constitución): common questions

Why is it called the Zócalo?

Zócalo means pedestal: a monument planned for the center in the 19th century never got beyond its base.

Why is it called Plaza de la Constitución?

It was named in 1813 after the Spanish Constitution of Cádiz of 1812, which was sworn here.

Can you see Diego Rivera's murals at the Zócalo?

Yes, inside the National Palace on the east side of the square; entry is free with an ID.
