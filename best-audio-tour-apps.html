Each app in a paragraph

VoiceMap

The closest thing to a podcast walk. Each of its 2,340 tours is written by a local (a historian, a novelist, a chef, a guide) and then shaped by VoiceMap's own script editors and sound engineers, and the audio starts by itself as you reach each point. You pick a tour before you set off and follow its route. Pick it if you want one polished, produced walk in a well-covered city. Skip it if you'd rather wander off-route.

Rick Steves Audio Europe

Free, and built from Rick Steves' guidebooks: walking tours of Europe's big sights plus interviews from his public-radio show, organized into playlists by place. You follow a map and press play yourself; nothing triggers by location. Pick it if you're in Europe and like his style. Skip it if you're outside Europe or want hands-free audio.

izi.TRAVEL

The biggest free catalogue: 25,000 tours in 2,500 cities, used by more than 3,000 museums as their official guide. Walking Mode plays stories as you approach attractions, and you can scan QR codes inside museums. Quality varies because many different organizations publish on it. Pick it if you're visiting museums or a city with an official tour. Skip it if the city you're in has thin coverage.

SmartGuide

Works with tourism boards and 6,000 local guides; stories autoplay while you walk, with offline maps and more than 30 languages. Some destinations open straight from a QR code in the browser. Pick it if your destination is one of its partner cities. Skip it if it isn't: coverage follows the partnerships.

GPSmyCity

More guidebook than audio guide: 7,000+ self-guided walks in 1,800+ cities with turn-by-turn directions and route maps that work without a data plan. You read about each stop rather than listen. Pick it if you want a planned route on a map. Skip it if you want someone talking in your ear.

Urban Tales (ours)

Instead of a catalogue of tours, Urban Tales tells you about whatever is around you: open the app, walk, and when you near a landmark the story starts on its own, even with the phone in your pocket. Stories are written for each landmark from verified sources (Wikipedia, plus the model's general knowledge) in historical, legends, fun-facts or cinematic style, in English, Spanish, Portuguese, French or German. You can also generate a route: a "7 Wonders" walk, a half day or a full day. Pick it if you like to wander, or you're somewhere no tour company has covered. Skip it if you'll be without data, or you want a hand-produced tour by a named local expert.

How to choose in one minute

  1. Do you want to press play, or have it start by itself? Hands-free: VoiceMap, izi.TRAVEL, SmartGuide, Urban Tales.
  2. Do you want a fixed route or to wander? Fixed: VoiceMap, GPSmyCity, Rick Steves. Wander: Urban Tales, izi.TRAVEL's Walking Mode.
  3. Will you have data? If not, download a VoiceMap, izi.TRAVEL, Rick Steves or GPSmyCity tour before you go.
  4. Is the city well covered? Big capitals have tours on every app. In smaller towns, check each app's map first; Urban Tales works anywhere it can find landmarks.

Questions people ask

What is the best free audio tour app?

For Europe, Rick Steves Audio Europe is free and well made. For museums and official city tours worldwide, izi.TRAVEL is free. Urban Tales gives 3 free landmarks a day in any city.

Which audio tour apps play automatically when you arrive?

VoiceMap, izi.TRAVEL (in Walking Mode), SmartGuide and Urban Tales start stories by GPS. Rick Steves Audio Europe and GPSmyCity are followed manually.

Which audio guide apps work offline?

VoiceMap, Rick Steves Audio Europe and izi.TRAVEL let you download tours; SmartGuide has offline maps; GPSmyCity's walks work without a data plan. Urban Tales needs a data connection.

Is there an audio guide app for any city, not just famous ones?

Catalogue apps depend on someone having made a tour of that place. Urban Tales writes stories for the landmarks around you, so it also works in towns no tour company covers.
