When you leave the script

Planned tours are great until you take a side street. Two things matter then: whether the app has anything to say about where you are, and whether it can help you decide what to do next.

  • Stories wherever you walk. Catalogue apps cover the places someone has written a tour for. Urban Tales tells the story of whatever landmark you're near, in a capital or a small town, and starts it by itself, even with your phone in your pocket.
  • Live practical info. On every landmark, Urban Tales shows whether it's open now, whether entry is free or paid, and the experiences nearby with their prices. SmartGuide includes useful tips in some of its tours, like entrance fees and the best time to visit; the other apps give you the story but no practical info.
  • A route in seconds. Urban Tales builds a walk on the spot: the city's 7 Wonders, a half day, a full day, several days, or your own pick of landmarks.

Each app, honestly

VoiceMap

Great at beautifully produced walks, written by local historians, novelists and guides and edited in-house. Tours download for offline use. Some tours are free.

Not great at languages: about 85% of its catalogue is in English. You buy one route at a time, and there's no practical information.

izi.TRAVEL

Great at museums: more than 3,000 use it as their official guide, with QR codes beside the exhibits. A huge catalogue with downloadable tours.

Not great at clarity: most city tours we found were paid, tour length isn't stated, and it's hard to tell which languages a tour comes in. No practical information.

Rick Steves Audio Europe

Great at knowledge: in-depth walks and interviews from one of the best-known travel writers, completely free and downloadable.

Not great at anything outside Europe. The app feels old-fashioned, nothing plays by location, some content can be dated, and there's no practical information.

SmartGuide

Great at well-prepared, pre-built guides, many languages, an AI chat to help plan your trip, and practical tips in some tours. Some tours play on their own as you walk.

Not great at places it hasn't produced a guide for. Language coverage varies a lot by destination, and it asks for several permissions up front.

GPSmyCity

Great at breadth on a budget: thousands of written walking routes with offline maps for $15–20 a year.

Not great at listening: there's no audio, so you read as you walk. Location only finds guides near you. The app feels dated, and we couldn't find where to change its language.

Urban Tales (ours)

Great at exploring without a plan: stories for every landmark around you, in any city, starting by themselves as you walk. Every landmark in 5 languages, live open/closed and entry info, experiences nearby, instant routes, and the lowest price for a whole city.

Not great at offline use: it needs a data connection. Stories are written by AI from verified sources and read by a synthetic voice, not recorded by a local guide. Only 5 languages, and as a newer app it has far fewer reviews than the others.

Using them together

You don't have to pick one. A good Rome day might look like this:

  • Morning: one signature VoiceMap walk through the ancient city, downloaded the night before.
  • Inside the Vatican Museums: the official audio guide or izi.TRAVEL, room by room.
  • Everything in between: Urban Tales in your pocket, telling you about the church, the fountain or the palazzo you just walked past, and whether the next one is open.

Questions people ask

Which audio tour app is cheapest for one city?

For a paid option, Urban Tales' City Unlock (about $5.99) covers every landmark in a city permanently. Rick Steves Audio Europe is free for the European cities it covers.

Which audio guide app works in my language everywhere?

Catalogue apps depend on tours having been made in your language for that city; VoiceMap's catalogue is about 85% English. Urban Tales tells every landmark in English, Spanish, Portuguese, French and German.

Which app tells me if a landmark is open and how much entry costs?

Urban Tales shows whether each landmark is open now, whether entry is free or paid, and nearby experiences with prices. SmartGuide includes entrance fees and best visiting times in some tours.

Which audio guide apps work offline?

VoiceMap, izi.TRAVEL and Rick Steves Audio Europe let you download tours, SmartGuide has offline maps, and GPSmyCity works without a data plan. Urban Tales needs a data connection.
