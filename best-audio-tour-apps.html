Self-guided audio tours · 2026

Most audio tour apps sell you a tour of a place someone already covered. Start from what you actually want out of the day, and the right app is usually obvious.

Who wrote this: the Urban Tales team, so yes, we're biased. Where another app is the better pick, we say so. Facts about other apps come from their own websites and store listings (October 2026).

What are you looking for?

"I'd rather wander than follow a route."

Urban Tales

Open the app and walk wherever you like. When you get near a landmark, its story starts on its own, and a short lead-in tells you where to look ("on your left, about 30 metres"). Put the phone in your pocket and keep going: Pocket Mode carries on in the background.

"Is it open, do I need a ticket, and what else is there to do?"

Urban Tales

Each landmark card shows whether the place is open now, whether entry is free or paid, and the tours and experiences available nearby with their starting prices, so you can decide on the spot instead of walking over to find the doors shut.

"I'm somewhere no guidebook bothers with."

Urban Tales

Catalogue apps only work where someone has produced a tour. Urban Tales writes stories for the landmarks around you, grounded in verified sources, so the same app works in Rome, in a mountain town, or on a stopover you didn't plan.

"I have one day and want a plan."

Urban Tales

Generate a walking route in seconds: the city's 7 Wonders, a half day, a full day, or your own pick of landmarks. Follow it, or drop it halfway and wander; the stories keep coming either way.

"I want it in my language, and told my way."

Urban Tales

Stories come in English, Spanish, Portuguese, French or German, and in four styles: historical, legends, fun facts or cinematic. The same building can be a history lesson in the morning and a ghost story at night.

"I'll be offline all day."

Download a tour before you go

Urban Tales needs a data connection. If you won't have one, VoiceMap, izi.TRAVEL and Rick Steves Audio Europe let you download tours in advance, and GPSmyCity's walking routes work without a data plan.

"I'm spending the day inside a museum."

izi.TRAVEL

More than 3,000 museums publish their official guides on it, with QR codes next to the exhibits. Use it inside; switch back to Urban Tales when you step out onto the street.

"I want one scripted walk, voiced by a local."

VoiceMap

Its tours are written by local writers and guides and produced in-house, like a podcast you walk through. You choose a tour in advance and follow its route.

The other apps, in one line each

  • VoiceMap: 2,340 produced tours in 600+ destinations; tours bought one by one or with a pass; downloadable.
  • izi.TRAVEL: free; 25,000 tours in 2,500 cities, many published by museums and tourism boards; quality varies by publisher.
  • Rick Steves Audio Europe: free guidebook walks and radio interviews for Europe; you press play yourself.
  • SmartGuide: guides made with tourism boards, 30+ languages; coverage follows its partner destinations.
  • GPSmyCity: 7,000+ written walking routes with offline maps; you read rather than listen.
