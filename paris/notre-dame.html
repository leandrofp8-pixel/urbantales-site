Three stories most visitors miss

1

The kings who lost their heads

In 1793 revolutionaries pulled down the 28 statues of kings on the facade, thinking they were kings of France; they were kings of Judah. Many heads were found in 1977, buried in a courtyard nearby, and are now in the Musée de Cluny.

2

Saved by a novel

By the 1830s the cathedral was in ruins. Victor Hugo's novel "The Hunchback of Notre-Dame" (1831) made Parisians care again, and Viollet-le-Duc restored it, adding the spire and many of the gargoyles you see.

3

The fire and the rebuild

On 15 April 2019 a fire destroyed the roof and the spire. It took five years and about 2,000 craftspeople to rebuild them, using oak and techniques close to the medieval ones; the cathedral reopened on 7 December 2024.

Notre-Dame: common questions

Is Notre-Dame open again?

Yes. It reopened on 7 December 2024, after five years of restoration following the 2019 fire. Entry to the cathedral is free.

How old is Notre-Dame?

Construction began in 1163 and was largely complete by the mid-14th century.

Who designed Notre-Dame's spire?

The spire that burned in 2019 was designed by Eugène Viollet-le-Duc in the 1850s and 1860s; it was rebuilt to the same design.
