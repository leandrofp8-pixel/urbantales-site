Three stories most visitors miss

1

The towers that don't match

Look up at the facade: the north tower was rebuilt by Chalgrin in the 1770s, the south tower was never finished. Money and the Revolution ran out first.

2

A sundial on the floor

A brass line crosses the floor to a marble obelisk. Sunlight through a small hole in a window lands on it at noon, which let the priests fix the exact date of the spring equinox, and so of Easter.

3

What the novel got wrong

"The Da Vinci Code" calls the line a pagan "Rose Line". The church put up a notice to set the record straight: it's a scientific instrument from the 18th century, nothing more mysterious.

Saint-Sulpice: common questions

What is the line on the floor of Saint-Sulpice?

A gnomon: a brass meridian line installed in the 1740s to measure the sun's position and fix the date of Easter.

Is Saint-Sulpice in The Da Vinci Code?

Yes, but the novel's "Rose Line" story is fiction; the church displays a notice explaining the gnomon's real purpose.

Why are Saint-Sulpice's towers different?

The north tower was rebuilt in the 1770s and the south tower was never completed.
