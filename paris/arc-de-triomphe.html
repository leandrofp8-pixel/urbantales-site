Three stories most visitors miss

1

A wooden arch for a wedding

When Napoleon married Marie-Louise in 1810, the arch had barely started. A full-size mock-up in wood and painted canvas was put up so the wedding procession could pass under it.

2

Hundreds of names

The inner walls carry the names of 128 battles and 660 officers of the Revolution and the Empire, most of them generals. Names that are underlined belong to men who died in battle.

3

The flame relit every evening

The Unknown Soldier from the First World War was buried under the arch in 1921. Since 1923 veterans' associations have rekindled the flame every evening at 6.30 pm, without missing a day, even during the German occupation.

TipUse the underground passage from the Champs-Élysées, never the roundabout, and be there at 6.30 pm for the rekindling of the flame. With Urban Tales running, the arch's story plays as you come up the stairs.

Arc de Triomphe: common questions

Who built the Arc de Triomphe?

Napoleon ordered it in 1806; it was designed by Jean Chalgrin and finished in 1836 under King Louis-Philippe.

What is under the Arc de Triomphe?

The Tomb of the Unknown Soldier from the First World War, with an eternal flame rekindled every evening at 6.30 pm.

Can you go to the top of the Arc de Triomphe?

Yes, there's a terrace at the top reached by 284 steps, with a ticket.
