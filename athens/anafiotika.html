Anafiotika: the history, and the islanders who built it

In the reign of King Otto, builders from the Cycladic island of Anafi came to work on the royal palace and built themselves homes on the slope of the Acropolis, just like the ones back home. Here's what you're looking at, and what to listen for in its lanes.

Three stories most visitors miss

1

Islanders in the capital

The workers came from Anafi, in the Cyclades, to help build King Otto's palace, today the Hellenic Parliament. Missing home, they built small, whitewashed, flat-roofed houses on the rock in the style of their island.

2

Smaller every decade

More islanders followed, and later refugees from Asia Minor after 1922. Parts of the neighbourhood were cleared for archaeological work in the 1950s, and the state bought many houses in the 1970s. About 45 houses remain.

3

People live here

Anafiotika isn't a museum. The lanes run past front doors and gardens, so walk quietly and respect the residents' privacy.

TipGo in from Stratonos Street above Plaka and follow the narrow paths uphill; getting a little lost is half the point. With Urban Tales running, the neighbourhood's story plays as you reach it.

Anafiotika: common questions

Where is Anafiotika?

On the northeast slope of the Acropolis, at the top of the Plaka district.

Who built Anafiotika?

Builders from the Cycladic island of Anafi, who came to Athens in the reign of King Otto to work on the royal palace.

Is Anafiotika worth visiting?

Yes, for a quiet half hour: whitewashed island houses, flowers and views over Athens, a few minutes from Plaka's busy streets.
