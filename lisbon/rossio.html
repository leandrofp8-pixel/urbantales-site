Three stories most visitors miss

1

One man, two crowns

The statue on the column is Pedro IV, King of Portugal and, as Pedro I, the first Emperor of Brazil, who declared Brazil's independence in 1822.

2

Markets, festivals and executions

For centuries the square hosted markets, festivals and bullfights, and in darker times the public executions of the Inquisition.

3

The wave under your feet

The black-and-white wave pavement, laid in the 1840s, was one of the first big designs in Portuguese stone pavement, the calçada portuguesa.

Rossio: common questions

Why is Rossio called Praça Dom Pedro IV?

That's its official name, after Pedro IV, whose statue tops the column; everyone still calls it Rossio.

Who was Dom Pedro IV?

King of Portugal and, as Pedro I, the first Emperor of Brazil, who declared Brazilian independence in 1822.

What is the wave pattern in Rossio?

Portuguese stone pavement (calçada portuguesa) laid in the 1840s, imitating the sea.
