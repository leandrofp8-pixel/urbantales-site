Three stories most visitors miss

1

Paid for by spices

Construction began in 1501, soon after Vasco da Gama returned from India, and was funded largely by a tax on the spices arriving from the East.

2

Monks who prayed for sailors

The Hieronymite monks who lived here prayed for the crews setting out from the Restelo beach nearby on their long voyages.

3

Two national heroes

Inside the church are the tombs of Vasco da Gama and of Luís de Camões, the poet who told the story of da Gama's voyage in The Lusiads.

Jerónimos Monastery: common questions

Who is buried in the Jerónimos Monastery?

Vasco da Gama and the poet Luís de Camões have tombs in the church, alongside kings of the Avis dynasty, including Manuel I.

Why was the Jerónimos Monastery built?

King Manuel I founded it to celebrate Portugal's sea voyages; the building began in 1501, after Vasco da Gama's return from India.

Did the Jerónimos Monastery survive the 1755 earthquake?

Yes, it came through with little damage, one of the few great buildings in Lisbon that did.
