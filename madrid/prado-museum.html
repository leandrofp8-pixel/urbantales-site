The Prado: a science museum that became an art museum

Juan de Villanueva's building was designed in the 18th century as a natural history cabinet. It opened in 1819 as a museum for the Spanish royal collection, and today holds the world's greatest collections of Velázquez and Goya.

Three stories most visitors miss

1

Meant for science

Charles III commissioned the building for natural history. Plans changed, and his grandson Ferdinand VII, encouraged by Queen Maria Isabel of Braganza, opened it as a royal museum of painting and sculpture in 1819.

2

A king's taste

The collection wasn't bought to cover all of art history. It's what Spanish kings collected, which is why it's so rich in Velázquez, Goya, El Greco, Titian and Rubens.

3

The Black Paintings

Goya painted the dark scenes known as the Black Paintings on the walls of his own house. They were later moved to canvas and are now in the Prado.

Prado Museum: common questions

When is the Prado free?

In the last two hours of opening on most days. Check the official site for current times.

What is the most famous painting in the Prado?

Velázquez's Las Meninas, along with Goya's Black Paintings and Bosch's Garden of Earthly Delights.

When did the Prado open?

In 1819, as the Royal Museum of Painting and Sculpture. It became national property after the revolution of 1868.
