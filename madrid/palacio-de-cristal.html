Palacio de Cristal: a greenhouse for an empire

Built of iron and glass in 1887 for an exhibition about the Philippines, then a Spanish colony, the Palacio de Cristal was a giant greenhouse for tropical plants. Today it's a free contemporary art space of the Reina Sofía museum.

Three stories most visitors miss

1

Made for plants

It was built to show tropical plants from the Philippines. The lake in front, with its swamp cypresses, was part of the same idea.

2

A president elected here

In 1936 the assembly that elected Manuel Azaña President of the Second Spanish Republic met here, because the parliament building was too small.

3

Tiles by Zuloaga

Look for the ceramic decoration by Daniel Zuloaga under the roofline, a detail most visitors miss while looking at the glass.

TipGo on a sunny morning, when the light through the glass is at its best, and walk round the lake to see it reflected. Entry is free. With Urban Tales running, its story plays as you reach the water.

Palacio de Cristal: common questions

Is the Palacio de Cristal free?

Yes. It hosts free contemporary art exhibitions of the Reina Sofía museum; it can close between shows and in bad weather.

Why was the Palacio de Cristal built?

For the 1887 Exhibition of the Philippine Islands, as a greenhouse for tropical plants.

Where is the Palacio de Cristal?

Inside Retiro Park, a short walk from the park's big pond.
