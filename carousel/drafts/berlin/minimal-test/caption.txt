East Germany spent decades trying to erase a giant cross from Berlin's skyline — and Egypt has spent over 100 years asking for a queen's head back. 👀

1. Brandenburg Gate
Napoleon had the Brandenburg Gate's bronze Quadriga dismantled and shipped to Paris as a war trophy in 1806. Prussian troops found it still sitting in its packing crates inside the Louvre in 1814 and hauled it straight home.

2. Berlin TV Tower
Sunlight hitting the TV Tower's steel panels forms a near-perfect cross, visible across the whole city. The officially atheist state tried paint, chemicals, and spotlights to kill the glare — nothing worked. Berliners still call it "the Pope's Revenge."

3. Reichstag
The Reichstag's glass dome, added in 1999, lets visitors walk a spiral ramp directly over the parliamentary chamber below — a deliberate statement that the public sits above its government. It's now the most-visited parliament building on Earth.

4. Berlin Wall
Within months of the Wall falling in 1989, 118 artists from 21 countries painted the East Side Gallery — now the longest surviving, and longest painted, stretch of the Berlin Wall anywhere on Earth.

5. Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church
RAF bombs gutted the church in 1943, leaving only its jagged spire standing. Instead of demolishing it, the city built a new church beside the ruin and kept it standing as a war memorial — still nicknamed the "Hohler Zahn" (hollow tooth).

6. Tempelhof Airport
When Tempelhof closed in 2008, the city planned apartments and a lake on its runways. In 2014, Berliners voted in a referendum to ban all construction — today it's one of the world's largest urban parks, roughly the size of Central Park.

7. Nefertiti Bust
The bust of Queen Nefertiti has sat in Berlin's Neues Museum since it left Egypt in 1913. Egypt calls the export illegal and has demanded its return for decades — even Hitler refused to give it up.
