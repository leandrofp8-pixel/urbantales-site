London's most famous clock tower isn't actually called Big Ben — and legend says the monarchy falls the day six ravens leave one particular fortress. 👀

1. Elizabeth Tower (Big Ben)
"Big Ben" is the nickname of the 13.5-tonne bell hanging inside the clock tower at the Palace of Westminster — the tower itself is officially the Elizabeth Tower, renamed in 2012 for the Queen's Diamond Jubilee.

2. Tower of London Ravens
Since Charles II's reign, the Tower of London has kept at least six ravens on site, guarding against an old legend that if they ever leave, the Crown itself will fall. Their wings are still clipped today, just in case.

3. Buckingham Palace
The Palace contains 775 rooms in total — including 92 offices, 78 bathrooms, and 188 staff bedrooms — making it one of the largest working royal residences still in use anywhere on Earth.

4. London Underground
The Metropolitan Railway between Paddington and Farringdon became the world's first underground passenger railway, running steam locomotives through smoke-filled tunnels more than 160 years ago.

5. Cleopatra's Needle
Beneath Cleopatra's Needle on the Victoria Embankment sits a sealed time capsule from 1878, packed with items like a box of cigars, a baby's bottle, and photographs of a dozen "pretty Englishwomen" of the day.

6. Postman's Park
Tucked inside Postman's Park, the Memorial to Heroic Self-Sacrifice holds dozens of Victorian ceramic tablets, each honoring an ordinary Londoner who died saving someone else's life.

7. Temple Church
Built by the Knights Templar in the 12th century, Temple Church still holds stone effigies of medieval knights on its floor — and later inspired a key scene in The Da Vinci Code.
