Three stories most visitors miss

1

Started on a lucky number

Charles IV is said to have laid the first stone at 5:31 am on 9 July 1357, chosen by astrologers because the date and time read as a palindrome: 1-3-5-7-9-7-5-3-1. Historians can't confirm it, but Prague loves the story.

2

The only bridge for 450 years

It replaced the Judith Bridge, washed away by a flood in 1342, and stayed the city's only crossing of the Vltava until 1841. It was simply called the Stone Bridge until 1870.

3

The saint thrown from the bridge

The statue of St John of Nepomuk marks the story of the priest thrown into the river in 1393. Legend says he refused to tell King Wenceslas IV what the queen had said in confession. Touching the bronze plaque below is supposed to bring you back to Prague, which is why it shines.

TipCross before 7 am to have the bridge almost to yourself, and look for the small cross on the parapet that marks where Nepomuk is said to have been thrown in. With Urban Tales running, the bridge's story plays as you step onto it.

Charles Bridge: common questions

How old is Charles Bridge?

Construction began in 1357 under Charles IV and was finished in the early 15th century.

Why do people touch the statue on Charles Bridge?

The bronze plaques under the statue of St John of Nepomuk are said to bring good luck and a return to Prague.

When is the best time to visit Charles Bridge?

Early morning, before 7 am, before the crowds and stalls arrive.
